Executive Abstract
TNF Finance operates in point-of-sale (POS) consumer installment lending across roughly 300 dealerships in southern Vietnam. The portfolio historically experienced a high default rate of 11.86%, burdened by slow and inconsistent manual underwriting. This study integrates six fragmented internal and external databases covering 20,000 loan applications while adhering strictly to Point-in-Time validation. An explainable Logistic Regression scorecard is established, yielding a ROC-AUC of 0.8262 and a Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) statistic of 0.4972 under Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation. Incorporating Credit Bureau records produced the largest single performance leap (+9.23% AUC), while cross-source metrics (Total Debt-to-Income and Income Exaggeration) provided critical resistance against application fraud. An operational cut-off threshold of 0.15 is proposed, enabling a 76.6% automated approval rate while more than halving post-approval bad debt to 5.34%.

1. Introduction & Business Problem

In retail installment financing (motorbikes, consumer electronics, and mobile phones), underwriting speed at the point of sale is a critical competitive advantage. However, retail loan applications inherently rely on self-declared customer submissions. Crucial determinants of actual creditworthiness—such as unverified earnings, external debt liabilities, and historical repayment discipline—are distributed across disparate legacy systems.

The objective of this assignment is to develop an automated, fully explainable credit scoring engine utilizing Logistic Regression to estimate the default probability of each incoming application upon submission.

2. Problem Formulation & Point-in-Time Principle

2.1. Analytical Unit and Target Label

  • Unit of Observation: A single loan application (ma_ho_so) captured at the time of store submission.
  • Target Variable (vo_no): Defined as vo_no = 1 if the borrower incurs 90 or more days past due (DPD ≥ 90) within the first 12 months on book, and 0 otherwise.
  • Dataset Scope: 16,046 labeled training observations (1,903 defaults, baseline bad rate of 11.86%) and 3,954 test records with withheld labels.

2.2. Strict Point-in-Time Information Principle

To prevent look-ahead bias and data leakage, all features must be computed strictly using information known on or prior to the application filing date (ngay_nop):

  • Historical Repayment Records (repayment_history.csv): Contains 179,265 historical payment periods. All scheduled dues concluded prior to December 1, 2023, while all incoming applications commenced in January 2024. Consequently, this source poses zero risk of temporal leakage.
  • Payroll Statements (payroll.csv): Covers 259,416 monthly payroll entries from July 2023 to June 2025, operating concurrently with applications. Temporal Guard: For each applicant, records were filtered strictly to thang ≤ thang(ngay_nop). Any earnings recorded subsequent to the application month were systematically excluded.

Treatment of Missing Segments:

  • Absence of Bureau Records (38.86%): Indicates no active debt at other registered institutions. Balances and overdue counts were assigned zero, alongside an indicator flag has_bureau = 0 (default rate: 5.79%).
  • First-Time Borrowers (59.92%): Applicants with no previous borrowing history at TNF were assigned zero for historical delinquency, identified via is_existing_cust = 0.
  • Informal Sector Workers (45.83%): Applicants lacking employer payroll linkages were flagged with has_payroll = 0, utilizing self-declared income as the proxy baseline.

4. Cross-Source Feature Engineering

The most potent predictors emerged from cross-referencing self-declarations against verified external records:

4.1. Total Debt-to-Income (Total DTI)

Total DTI = (New TNF Monthly Payment + External Bureau Monthly Debt) / Estimated Monthly Income

Empirical Finding: Applicants in the highest DTI quartile (> 37%) exhibited a default rate of 23.01%, compared to just 5.18% in the lowest quartile (≤ 14.3%). Total DTI captures aggregate household leverage that siloed appraisals routinely miss.

4.2. Income Exaggeration Factor

Income Exaggeration = 1 - (Latest Verified Payroll Salary / Self-Declared Stated Income)

Empirical Finding: For applicants with verified payroll, exaggerating declared earnings by over 33% resulted in a default surge to 22.32% (versus 5.66% among accurate disclosers). This factor unmasks moral hazard during loan origination.

4.3. Equity and Delinquency Features

  • Down-Payment Ratio (tra_truoc / gia_tri_hang): Reflects customer commitment; higher equity significantly lowers default risk.
  • Historical Delinquency (repay_dpd_max): Past payment tardiness at TNF remains a durable indicator of future recidivism.

7. Practical Insights, Limitations & Maintenance

7.1. Empirical Risk Findings

  • Repeat Borrowers Are Not Inherently Safer: Existing TNF clients demonstrated a higher aggregate default rate (13.23% vs. 10.94% for new borrowers). While pristine historical clients (DPD = 0) represent prime profiles, repeat borrowers who struggled in prior contracts frequently refinance to roll over distressed debt. Tenure should never warrant automatic approval.
  • Product-Specific Risk Heterogeneity: Motorbike financing exhibits the highest default rate (13.58%) due to larger ticket sizes and long commitments (18–24 months). Conversely, mobile phones demonstrate the lowest delinquency (10.70%) due to shorter durations and irreplaceable utility in daily livelihoods.

7.2. Limitations & Model Governance

  • Informal Sector Underwriting: Approximately 46% of retail borrowers lack formal payroll verification. TNF should explore alternative data sources (telecom credit scoring and utility history) to supplement income estimation.
  • Stability Monitoring: Track the Population Stability Index (PSI) monthly on critical features (dti_tong, bureau_tong_du_no). If PSI exceeds 0.25 (indicating distribution drift), model recalibration should be scheduled immediately.
